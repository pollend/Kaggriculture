Executive assessment

The agent has a sound core design: rules enumerate field work and manage navigation and markets, while a small neural network ranks unit/job pairs. That keeps inference fast, makes Kaggle packaging straightforward, and reduces the learning problem to a tractable action-selection task. The submission boundary and feature parity tests are especially strong.

Before a multi-hour training run, three issues deserve priority: replay does not preserve the action mask used at inference, a fresh persistent store does not initialize from the shipped model, and persistent training uses exploration and replay settings that the standalone trainer documents as unstable.

Prioritized findings

1

Farm hands can move when the policy selects PASS

High

The only pass candidate targets the main farmer's position. For a hired hand, the routing layer sees a remote target and moves the hand toward the farmer instead of doing nothing.

Sources: jobs.py, lines 73–75 · main.py, lines 141–149

Recommendation: bypass routing for PASS, or generate a pass candidate at each acting unit's current position.

2

Bellman targets can use jobs unavailable to the acting unit

High

Inference masks jobs already reserved by earlier units and jobs owned by another unit. The rollout does not store that per-unit mask, so replay treats every real candidate row as eligible when maximizing the next-state Q-value. This can overestimate contested jobs and owner-specific actions such as DROP.

Sources: main.py, lines 97–137 · training/replay.py, lines 145–159

Recommendation: persist the exact allowed mask for each unit decision and apply it during online selection and target-network maximization.

3

A fresh persistent database ignores the shipped checkpoint

High

The persistent updater creates a random network when the database has no active DQN checkpoint. Its first evaluated candidate can then become active without competing against weights.npz, allowing a new training store to replace a stronger shipped model.

Sources: training/updater.py, lines 48–57 and 255–264 · safer standalone behavior in training/train.py, lines 134–142

Recommendation: import weights.npz as the initial active checkpoint and measured incumbent when creating a new experiment store.

4

Persistent defaults restore settings associated with collapse

High

The manifest uses a 200-episode replay buffer and a 0.05 epsilon floor. The standalone trainer documents catastrophic forgetting with the smaller buffer and policy degeneration with the lower exploration floor; it uses 800 and 0.15 instead.

Sources: agent.toml, lines 34 and 41 · training/train.py, lines 41–52

Recommendation: begin persistent training with buffer_episodes = 800 and epsilon_end = 0.15, then tune from measured arena results.

5

Opponent identity is correlated with player seat

Medium

Opponent selection and learner seat both derive from the episode index. With an even opponent list, each opponent repeatedly sees the learner in the same seat, allowing seat effects to become entangled with opponent identity.

Source: training/collect.py, lines 293–309

Recommendation: collect both orientations for each opponent or independently shuffle seat assignment every batch.

6

Some generated FERTILIZE jobs are impossible

Medium

Candidate generation counts fertilizer in the shed and across every unit inventory, but execution consumes fertilizer from the acting unit. A unit can therefore select a fertilize job when the item is in the shed or held by someone else, producing a silent no-op.

Source: jobs.py, lines 79–105

Recommendation: bind fertilizer availability to the acting unit, or add explicit pickup and transport jobs.

7

The two training entry points have materially different behavior

Medium

The standalone trainer and persistent updater differ in replay size, exploration, opponents, checkpoint formats, incumbent initialization, and promotion gates. The effective meaning of “trained DQN” therefore depends on which command launched it.

Sources: training/train.py · training/updater.py · agent.toml

Recommendation: share one configuration, trainer core, incumbent bootstrap, and promotion policy between both workflows.

Inference architecture

This is a hybrid controller rather than end-to-end reinforcement learning. Deterministic code owns job enumeration, routing, commitment, and market orders. The neural network only ranks candidate field jobs for each unit.

Sequential assignment

Units choose one at a time. Earlier selections reserve their jobs, and later units receive an updated mask. This helps prevent two workers from targeting the same tile, but makes storing the exact replay mask essential.

Sticky commitments

A selected remote job remains active until arrival, invalidation, or the commitment timeout. Commitment state is included in the feature vector, preventing the policy from changing destinations on every movement step.

Reward

Training combines money changes, potential-based shaping, and a terminal outcome bonus. The shaped return gives the learner denser feedback than season-end money alone.

Replay and targets

The trainer uses a recent-plus-reservoir episode buffer, n-step returns, Double DQN, and a target network. Serialization uses compact arrays rather than pickle.

Curriculum

Standalone training progresses from starter opponents to a crop heuristic and frozen self-play snapshots. Persistent collection can draw from the larger configured league.

Evaluation

Promotion is based on paired score margin rather than raw money, which is important because opponent production changes the shared market and depresses both players' absolute earnings.

Credit-assignment caveat. Step-level joint reward is assigned to sampled transitions for individual units, and committed movement turns are represented like fresh choices. Recording only selection points—or using option-duration returns—would align the learning data more closely with deployed behavior.

Learned scope and strategic ceiling

Currently scored

  • PASS
  • WATER
  • HARVEST
  • PLANT
  • DIG
  • FERTILIZE
  • DROP

Outside the current policy

  • Buying and exploiting additional land
  • Coops, pastures, and animal placement
  • Feeding, care, and animal production
  • Collecting animal fertilizer
  • General inventory pickup and delivery
  • Learned market purchasing and liquidation

The crop-only boundary is appropriate for an initial DQN and reduces exploration cost, but the README's phrase “every legal job” is broader than the implemented action space. Animals, land expansion, and richer logistics will eventually set the performance ceiling.

What is already strong

Clean submission boundary

Inference uses NumPy and local code only. Training and PyTorch are excluded from the submission allowlist, and bundle tests import the unpacked agent away from the repo.

Single-sourced features

Training and inference share feature construction, while tests verify parity between the PyTorch and NumPy network implementations.

Practical movement behavior

Sticky commitments address target oscillation, and the commitment state is visible to the model instead of becoming hidden controller state.

Measured promotion

The project recognizes that training is non-monotonic and evaluates checkpoints before shipping. Immutable checkpoints and rollout artifacts improve reproducibility.

Validation status. The focused DQN, bundle, and game-fact tests pass. The existing tests do not currently cover farm-hand pass behavior, replay availability masks, or fresh-database checkpoint bootstrapping.

Recommended implementation order

  1. Store and enforce per-unit action-availability masks in replay.
  2. Bootstrap a new persistent store from the shipped weights.npz.
  3. Raise persistent replay capacity to 800 episodes and epsilon floor to 0.15.
  4. Make PASS unit-local so hired hands remain stationary.
  5. Balance every opponent across both player seats.
  6. Make fertilizer candidates aware of the acting unit's inventory.
  7. Unify standalone and persistent configuration, training, and promotion logic.
  8. Optimize historical artifact loading before the database grows large.

With the first six items resolved, this architecture is a credible foundation for long crop-policy training runs. Expanding into animals and land should come afterward so action correctness and checkpoint safety are stable before the search space grows.
