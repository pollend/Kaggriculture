Configurable Mixed-Farm Surrogate

A deterministic, full-economy policy that recalculates a target portfolio every turn, converts it into field tasks, assigns those tasks to workers, and issues budgeted market orders. Offline, a league and Random Forest surrogate search the same versioned parameter space.

At a glance

One policy, two separate loops

Live match

Rules and greedy decisions

Loads profiles/balanced.json, rebuilds the economic plan every turn, and emits deterministic unit actions and market orders.

Offline league

Evolution plus a surrogate model

Samples, mutates, and crosses strategies; a Random Forest ranks candidate parameter sets and is promoted only when held-out error does not regress.

The word “surrogate” does not mean inference happens during gameplay. The submitted agent never loads the Random Forest. The model helps the local league propose promising configurations; shipping one still requires a reviewed change to balanced.json.

This is not a beam-search planner or reinforcement-learning controller. It is a configurable heuristic policy with a clean split between what assets should the farm own? (portfolio), what physical work is needed? (task enumeration), who should do it? (assignment), and what should be traded? (market policy).

Runtime architecture

What happens on every turn

1 · ObserveFarm, private inventory, prices, town, clock
2 · PlanTarget crops, herd, labor, reserves, land
3 · EnumerateCare, harvest, logistics, build, plant, drop
4 · AssignEmergency first, commitments, then utility
5 · EmitFarmer, hands, up to 10 market orders
  1. main.py creates one callable from the checked-in balanced profile.
  2. ConfigurableMixedFarmAgent.__call__ validates the observation, detects a new episode, and reads runtime values such as season length and hire-cost multiplier.
  3. build_plan recomputes economic targets from time remaining, current prices, town demand, opponent supply, owned assets, cash, and strategy parameters.
  4. market_orders independently constructs an ordered, cash-aware list of sales and purchases.
  5. enumerate_tasks turns the current board and portfolio gaps into scored, deadline-bearing physical tasks.
  6. assign_actions gives each unit at most one action, retaining travel commitments across turns.

Episode-local state contains only the player, last step, operation counts, and per-unit task commitments. It resets on step 0, player changes, or non-increasing step numbers; no match data persists in submission-time globals.

Economic layer

The greedy remaining-season portfolio

The planner scores every crop and animal without simulating paths or individual future turns. Scores represent an approximate remaining-season economic value adjusted by configured preferences, public demand, opponent supply, and labor or risk.

Crop scoring

score = ((revenue × demand_boost ÷ supply_discount − seed_costs) × crop_weight) ÷ labor
  • If there is not enough time to reach first yield, the score becomes negative seed cost.
  • One-time crops estimate repeated cycles using peak_day + 1; ongoing crops use their configured cumulative yield once.
  • Town shops boost demanded products; visible opponent production discounts crowded products.
  • The configured number of crop tiles is divided proportionally among positive scores using largest-remainder allocation.

Animal scoring

net = (product_revenue × demand_boost ÷ supply_discount + fertilizer_value − feed_cost − animal_cost) ÷ risk_premium

Product revenue includes an approximate care multiplier, while daily wheat and expected fertilizer are priced at current market values. A species receives its configured target count only when its unweighted net is positive; its weight controls relative placement priority, not that profitability gate.

Feed self-sufficiency

Wheat is forced toward ceil(total animals / 0.8) tiles, displacing lower-scoring crops as needed.

Labor target

Daily care load is converted into hands with roughly 10 estimated useful actions per worker, then capped by max_hands.

Reserves

Wheat reserve equals herd size × reserve days. Fertilizer reserve is capped at 6 and grows with planted area.

Land expansion gate

Buying the next quadrant requires every condition below:

  • the configured quadrant cap has not been reached;
  • the desired portfolio exceeds current tile capacity;
  • current assets and pending seeds/animals substantially commit the existing land (at least 80%, with a floor of 20);
  • cash after the configured reserve covers the land price; and
  • estimated marginal value of the added tiles clears price × land_hurdle.

Operational layer

Tasks, deadlines, and joint assignment

A task records its kind, board target, executable action, approximate coin value, deadline, optional unit owner, and optional required carried item. The scheduler generates a fresh task frontier every turn.

Three assignment passes

Pass 1
Emergencies
Tasks worth at least 20,000 reserve only the workers needed for immediate survival or terminal logistics. Utility is value minus travel cost.
Pass 2
Commitments
A worker continues toward its prior task if that exact task still exists and its inventory still satisfies the requirement. This reduces turn-to-turn thrashing.
Pass 3
Greedy utility
Remaining workers and unique tasks are paired by value + deadline urgency − Manhattan travel penalty.
utility = task_value + deadline_weight × urgency(slack) − 125 × movement_cost × Manhattan_distance

Negative deadline slack adds a large urgency penalty correction; otherwise urgency decays with available slack. If a worker is already at the target it executes the task action, otherwise it moves horizontally first and then vertically on later turns. Each task can be assigned only once per turn.

Market layer

Strictly ordered and capped at ten

Orders are assembled in this order, so earlier economic needs win when the per-turn cap is reached:

  1. Sell shed products when liquidating, cash-starved, above the configured base-price ratio, or under shed pressure.
  2. Buy land when the portfolio gate passes and cash above reserve can cover it.
  3. Hire hands early in the day until the plan target or configured cap is reached.
  4. Buy emergency/operating wheat for the current herd. This can spend below the normal cash reserve.
  5. Buy animals by descending animal score, using only the remaining strategic budget.
  6. Buy seeds by descending crop score when enough season remains for first yield.
Reserves change with the clock. Before the final day, wheat is retained for the herd. Fertilizer is retained until liquidation. On the final day, sale logic stops protecting both because unsold inventory has no terminal value.

Season behavior

Build, unwind, liquidate

Normal operationBuy assets, expand land, plant, build, feed, care, fertilize, harvest, and sell opportunistically.
Liquidation windowDefault: final 72 turns. Stop new structures and planting; sell shed goods regardless of price and stop buying land, animals, or seeds.
Final dayFinal 24 turns. Strongly prioritize returning carried saleable output and stop protecting wheat/fertilizer reserves.

Field logistics and market liquidation reinforce one another: terminal DROP tasks get a large value boost so carried output reaches the shed, and market orders then sell shed contents. Animals themselves remain excluded from carried-output value because they are not saleable products.

Model representation

How decisions are encoded

The Random Forest does not encode individual actions such as WATER, BUY_SEED, or MOVE. It encodes an entire Strategy as one flat numeric feature vector: one column for each of the 30 configuration values. An evaluated strategy becomes one training row, paired with an outcome score derived from its league results.

Strategy object
max_hands=5, target_cow=6, melon_weight=1.4, movement_cost=0.8, …
Feature row
A flat mapping produced by Strategy.to_features(). Integers and floats remain numeric; there are no categorical or one-hot fields.
Training label
A scalar quality target: 45% conservative rating, 35% mean margin, and 20% final money after within-generation standardization.

The forest learns cuts through parameter space

Yes: each decision tree repeatedly makes an axis-aligned cut on a single parameter. A simplified path might look like this:

target_cow > 4.5 → movement_cost ≤ 1.05 → liquidation_turns > 55.5 → predicted strategy quality

Integer settings can have half-step thresholds because scikit-learn treats every feature as numeric. Continuous weights can be cut at any learned midpoint. Each leaf stores the average training target of rows that reached it; the Random Forest prediction is the average prediction across its trees. With 300 trees fitted on bootstrap samples, the ensemble can approximate interactions and nonlinear regions—for example, “larger cow targets work only when wheat, labor, and liquidation settings are also in compatible ranges.”

Core forest

Maps the 30 strategy parameters to overall league quality. It is trained from rating, margin, and final-money outcomes.

Optional Moon forest

Uses the same 30 columns but predicts margin specifically against the Moon Counts Melons family.

How a model prediction becomes a proposal

  1. Randomly sample 10,000 valid strategy points from the bounded schema.
  2. Encode all points into a 10,000 × 30 feature table in the checkpoint's recorded column order.
  3. Predict core quality for every point; if the Moon model exists, standardize both prediction streams and blend them 70% / 30%.
  4. Sort by predicted score and return the requested highest-ranked candidates to the league.
  5. The league still evaluates those candidates in actual games. The prediction itself never directly promotes a strategy or chooses an in-game action.
Two levels of “decision” are easy to confuse. Offline, tree cuts choose which parameter regions look promising. Online, ordinary Python formulas and greedy task assignment turn the selected parameter vector into concrete farm actions.

Offline learning loop

Evolution proposes; the league judges

1 · Compete20 strategies play 24 rounds/generation
2 · RecordClosed generation becomes a training batch
3 · FitRandom Forest learns parameter → outcome
4 · GateNewest generation is held out
5 · ProposeRank 10,000 sampled strategies

Population operators

  • Sample: draw every parameter within its declared valid range.
  • Mutate: replace 1 to roughly ¼ of fields with a blend of the parent and a random donor.
  • Crossover: average or select integer fields, average float fields, then mutate the child.
  • Model proposal: when an active checkpoint exists, ignore the supplied parent list, sample the candidate pool, score it, and return the highest-ranked requested count.

Core training target

For each generation, three outcomes are standardized within that generation and combined:

target = 0.45 × z(conservative rating) + 0.35 × z(mean margin) + 0.20 × z(final money)

A 300-tree Random Forest (default, leaf minimum 2) learns this core target. When at least 20 distinct strategies have games against the “Moon Counts Melons” family, a second forest learns that head-to-head margin. Candidate ranking blends standardized core and Moon predictions at 70% / 30% by default.

Checkpoint gate and ownership

  • At least two closed generation batches and six rated strategies are required.
  • All generations except the newest train the candidate; the newest validates it.
  • The candidate is promoted only when weighted normalized MAE is no worse than the active checkpoint (or no incumbent exists).
  • The persisted checkpoint is refit on all available rows and stored as a Joblib artifact with a SHA-256 digest.
  • The league owns matchups, batches, checkpoints, and promotion. The strategy owns encoding, variation, candidate ranking, and model fitting.
Training promotion is not release promotion. The active checkpoint affects future league proposals. It does not rewrite profiles/balanced.json or silently change the Kaggle submission.

Evidence and diagnostics

How behavior is measured

After an episode is committed, evaluate_episode reads the stored summary and replay and emits family-specific diagnostics: animal purchases and final herd, land purchases, feed/care/fertilizer actions, worst observed unfed streak, product sales, final money and margin, action mix, inferred plant loss, and unsold terminal inventory.

Regression tests

Cover strategy ID stability, feed-aware portfolio construction, land gating, animal logistics, reserve-breaking feed purchases, terminal behavior, action shape, episode reset, and a profitable full season.

Acceptance arena

Paired-seat series compare the mixed policy with the crop-only surrogate against Moon Counts Melons, with an optional broad competitor sweep and 95% confidence intervals.

The default isolated runner uses 48 generations, population 20, 24 rounds per generation, 12 Hall-of-Fame rounds, 720-step episodes, seed 2718, and a 10,000-strategy proposal pool.

Interpretation notes

What the design trades away

  • No lookahead search: asset values are coarse remaining-season estimates, so the policy does not simulate congestion, exact action chains, or market trajectories.
  • Greedy assignment: workers are paired one task at a time; this is fast and deterministic but not a globally optimal multi-worker schedule.
  • Simple routing: movement is Manhattan, horizontal-first, and ignores future traffic because workers may cross locked tiles.
  • Approximate economics: crop cycles, animal care value, fertilizer yield, labor load, and wheat productivity use fixed estimates rather than an exact simulator.
  • Hard-coded game assumptions: several constants mirror the default environment, including 25 tiles per quadrant and shed capacity 100.
  • One inert schema knob: emergency_multiplier is sampled and validated but is not currently referenced by the runtime scheduler; emergency values are fixed constants.
  • No automatic release: training can improve candidate selection without changing the checked-in submission profile.

The resulting policy is best understood as a transparent, tunable baseline for the complete farm economy: much richer than a single-crop loop, far cheaper and easier to inspect than receding-horizon search, and structured so league data can improve its configuration without putting training state inside the agent.
